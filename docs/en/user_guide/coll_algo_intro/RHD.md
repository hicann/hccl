## Algorithm Description

As the network scales up, for example, to 4K ranks, it becomes difficult for Mesh to form a fully connected network among all ranks (though a full connection would complete the operation in a single clock cycle). Moreover, the resource overhead—including link, switch, and synchronization resources—is excessively high, and there may be a mismatch between computational power and resource consumption. Ring, on the other hand, is more resource-efficient (each node only sends and receives from its left and right neighbors), but it requires too many steps and suffers from slow progression. In large-scale clusters, where intra-server data volume is huge and the Ring is extremely long, Ring's data-splitting approach no longer offers an advantage.

The RHD (Recursive Halving-Doubling) algorithm performs data exchange between NPUs through recursive doubling and recursive halving. Compared with Mesh, it consumes fewer resources, and compared with Ring, it offers higher efficiency.

![](figures/rhd.png)

The implementation process of the RHD algorithm is illustrated in the figure above. Assuming there are 5 (2<sup>2</sup>+1) ranks, the data of rank1 is first merged into rank0, reducing the number of ranks to 4 (2<sup>2</sup>). Then, the data of these 4 ranks is exchanged and summed in pairs through halving, which is the ReduceScatter operation. In the next phase, the data of these 4 ranks is concatenated in pairs, which is the AllGather operation. Finally, the data of rank0 is replicated to rank1, at which point each rank holds the full sum of data from all ranks.

The RHD algorithm is also applicable to star or fat-tree topology interconnections, and its time complexity is $\lceil log_{2}N \rceil$.

## Time Consumption Calculation

Recursive Halving-Doubling is a recursive halving and doubling algorithm. For an integer power of 2, the Vector/Distance Halving/Doubling strategy is used. For a non-integer power of 2, the ranks are divided into two parts: 2r (part1) and p-2r, where $r=p-2^{\lfloor log(p)\rfloor}$. First, the part1 portion is merged into r, so that the sum of the remaining ranks is p-r (block). Then the HD (Halving-Doubling) algorithm for an integer power of 2 is executed. Finally, 2r is restored from part1 to obtain the final result.
